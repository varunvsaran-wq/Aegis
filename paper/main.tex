\begin{abstract}
% NOTE: every number in this abstract is a PLACEHOLDER (\NUM{}); fill from
% the MLflow runs that also generate the report/ tables and figures.
Large language models used for retrieval-augmented question answering vary
widely in cost, yet the reliability gap between cheap and frontier models is
often attributable to fragile scaffolding rather than raw model capability.
We present \emph{Aegis}, a model-agnostic reliability harness that wraps a
hybrid retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) core in four stages: a query
structurer that emits a typed, decomposed retrieval plan; a prompt-injection
defense combining a direct heuristic gate with indirect spotlighting,
imperative stripping, and canary-token exfiltration detection; a hybrid
retrieval core (dense \texttt{bge-base-en-v1.5} + BM25 fused with Reciprocal
Rank Fusion and reranked by a \texttt{bge-reranker-v2-m3} cross-encoder) under
a strict citation contract; and a natural-language-inference (NLI)
groundedness verifier with a retry-once-then-abstain loop and $k$-sample
self-consistency voting. The harness is orchestrated as a LangGraph state
machine and evaluated across a four-model price frontier (local
\texttt{llama3.1:8b}, \texttt{claude-haiku}, \texttt{gpt-4o},
\texttt{claude-opus}) on HotpotQA with statistical rigor: $\geq 3$ seeds,
bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals, paired McNemar and Wilcoxon tests, and
Benjamini--Hochberg correction. On \NUM{N} multi-hop questions, the harness
improves exact match by \NUM{$\Delta$EM} points
(95\% CI [\NUM{lo}, \NUM{hi}], McNemar $p<\NUM{p}$) and lets an 8B model
approach frontier-raw accuracy at roughly \NUM{X}$\times$ lower cost per
query, while reducing indirect prompt-injection success from \NUM{A}\% to
\NUM{B}\%. We release the evaluation CLI, an adversarial poisoned-corpus
generator, and a per-component ablation study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation \citep{lewis2020rag} has become the default
recipe for grounding language models in external knowledge, but deployed RAG
systems remain unreliable in three orthogonal ways: they hallucinate claims
their retrieved context does not support, they answer inconsistently across
samples, and they are vulnerable to prompt injection carried inside retrieved
documents \citep{greshake2023injection}. Practitioners often respond by
reaching for a more expensive model, conflating the reliability gap with a
capability gap.

This paper asks a different question: \emph{how much of the reliability gap
between a cheap model and a frontier model can be closed by the harness around
the model, rather than the model itself?} We operationalize the question with
Aegis, a fixed, model-agnostic pipeline that any LiteLLM-compatible model can
be dropped into, and we measure the effect on grounded multi-hop QA.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A four-stage reliability harness (query structuring, injection
  defense, hybrid retrieval under a citation contract, and NLI groundedness
  verification with self-consistency voting), implemented as a LangGraph state
  machine and described in Section~\ref{sec:method}.
  \item A cost-controlled evaluation across a four-model price frontier on
  HotpotQA, showing that a cheap model plus the harness can approach
  frontier-raw accuracy after accounting for harness overhead
  (Section~\ref{sec:e2}), with the ``cheap+harness $\approx$ frontier-raw''
  claim backed by a paired significance test rather than chart proximity.
  \item A robustness study on a poisoned corpus reporting attack success rate
  (ASR) per attack category, raw versus harnessed
  (Section~\ref{sec:e3}), plus a released poisoned-corpus generator.
  \item A per-component ablation isolating the contribution of the structurer,
  reranker, and verifier, each with its own paired significance test and a
  family-wise multiple-comparison correction (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}

runs into \texttt{report/}; no result is hand-transcribed.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Multi-hop QA benchmarks.}
HotpotQA \citep{yang2018hotpotqa} provides multi-hop, Wikipedia-based
questions with sentence-level supporting-fact annotations, which we exploit
for objective citation-precision scoring. MuSiQue \citep{trivedi2022musique}
increases hop count, and CRAG \citep{yang2024crag} stresses noisy and
misleading retrieval; we use HotpotQA as the primary benchmark and discuss
the others as extensions.

\paragraph{Hybrid retrieval and reranking.}
Dense passage retrieval \citep{karpukhin2020dpr} and sparse BM25 scoring
\citep{robertson2009bm25} are complementary; Reciprocal Rank Fusion
\citep{cormack2009rrf} combines their rankings without score calibration. We
use BGE embeddings \citep{xiao2024cpack} for the dense arm and a BGE
cross-encoder reranker built on the DeBERTa architecture
\citep{he2021deberta}.

\paragraph{Faithfulness and consistency.}
RAGAS \citep{es2024ragas} formalizes reference-free RAG evaluation
(faithfulness, context precision/recall). We instead verify groundedness with
a lightweight NLI cross-encoder \citep{he2021deberta} at the level of each
cited chunk, and reduce answer variance with self-consistency majority voting
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}.

\paragraph{Prompt injection.}
Indirect prompt injection embeds adversarial instructions in retrieved
content \citep{greshake2023injection}. Spotlighting
\citep{hines2024spotlighting} and instruction-hierarchy training
\citep{wallace2024instructionhierarchy} are two defenses; Aegis adopts
spotlighting-style delimiting plus imperative stripping and canary-token
exfiltration detection at the retrieval boundary.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

A query flows through four harness stages wrapping a hybrid-RAG core. The
whole pipeline is a stateful LangGraph graph
(\texttt{structure}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{retrieve}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{generate}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{verify}
with conditional \texttt{generate\_retry}/\texttt{abstain} edges), compiled
once and reused per question. The pipeline is model-agnostic: the generation,
structuring, and verification-retry calls all go through a single LiteLLM
gateway (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) that also performs token and cost
accounting.

\subsection{Stage 1: Query Structurer}
\label{sec:structurer}
The structurer makes one LLM call that rewrites the raw question into a typed
\texttt{StructuredQuery} validated by pydantic, with fields \emph{intent}, a
list of 1--4 decomposed \emph{sub\_questions}, retrieval \emph{keywords}, and
an \emph{answer\_type} drawn from
\{\texttt{short\_phrase}, \texttt{yes\_no}, \texttt{entity},
\texttt{number}, \texttt{date}\}. Parsing is tolerant: any malformed model
output degrades to a safe fallback (the original question as the sole
sub-question) so the pipeline never crashes on structurer noise. Decomposition
drives \emph{multi-query retrieval}: the original question and each
sub-question are retrieved for separately, and the per-chunk results are
merged keeping the maximum rerank score. Schema-enforced output is the
consistency mechanism --- it constrains the answer surface before generation.

\subsection{Stage 2: Injection Defense}
\label{sec:defense}
Two gates guard the retrieval boundary. The \emph{direct} gate classifies the
incoming user query with a heuristic detector (optionally backed by a
DeBERTa injection-classifier baseline). The \emph{indirect} defense sanitizes
each retrieved chunk before it enters the prompt: spotlighting delimiters mark
retrieved text as untrusted data rather than instructions
\citep{hines2024spotlighting}, imperative-pattern stripping removes injected
directives, and a canary token planted in the system prompt is monitored so
that any attempt to exfiltrate it triggers an automatic block. Poisoned chunks
are tagged in the vector store metadata so clean and poisoned corpora can be
isolated for the robustness experiment (Section~\ref{sec:e3}).

\subsection{Stage 3: Hybrid RAG Core with a Citation Contract}
\label{sec:rag}
Retrieval is hybrid: a dense arm (\texttt{bge-base-en-v1.5}, L2-normalized
cosine over a Qdrant index, with an in-memory fallback) and a sparse BM25 arm
each return their top candidates, whose ranks are fused with Reciprocal Rank
Fusion \citep{cormack2009rrf},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RRF}(d) = \sum_{r \in \text{rankings}} \frac{1}{k + \mathrm{rank}_r(d)},
\qquad k = 60,
\end{equation}
after which a \texttt{bge-reranker-v2-m3} cross-encoder rescores the fused
candidate pool and the top $k_{\text{final}}$ chunks are kept. Generation runs
under a strict \emph{citation contract}: the model must emit exactly two lines,
\texttt{ANSWER:} and \texttt{CITATIONS:} (comma-separated chunk ids copied from
the context markers). Citations to ids outside the retrieved set are dropped;
an answer that cites nothing triggers one enforcement retry and, if still
uncited, is rejected as an abstention. This makes every accepted claim
traceable to a retrieved chunk.

\subsection{Stage 4: Groundedness Verifier and Consistency Vote}
\label{sec:verify}
Each accepted answer is checked by an NLI cross-encoder
(\texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base}): the answer is rephrased as a
declarative claim (hypothesis) and each cited chunk's text is a premise; the
answer is \emph{grounded} iff at least one cited chunk entails the claim. On a
verification failure, the contradiction summary is fed back and the model
re-answers exactly once; a second failure yields an abstention rather than an
unsupported answer. Orthogonally, self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}
is implemented as $k$-sample majority voting: $k$ answers are sampled,
clustered by the official HotpotQA answer normalization, and the largest
cluster wins, with the cluster fraction logged as an agreement/confidence
score. The retry/abstain loop and the vote are the conditional edges of the
LangGraph state machine.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

% NOTE: this is the reproducibility section reviewers scrutinize. Keep it in
% exact sync with aegis/config.py (MODEL_REGISTRY, embedder/reranker/nli
% defaults, RRF_K) and aegis/eval/run.py (logged params).

\paragraph{Models and versions.}
Four models span the price frontier, addressed through a LiteLLM gateway and
identified by registry alias / exact provider string:
\emph{local} = \texttt{ollama/llama3.1:8b} (\$0 marginal cost),
\emph{small} = \texttt{anthropic/claude-haiku-4-5},
\emph{mid} = \texttt{openai/gpt-4o}, and
\emph{frontier} = \texttt{anthropic/claude-opus-4-8}. The gateway records
tokens in/out, per-call USD cost (via LiteLLM's cost model; local models are
\$0), and latency for every call, including harness-internal calls
(structuring, verification retries, and $k$-vote samples), so reported cost is
the \emph{net} cost of the harness, not just the final generation.

\paragraph{Benchmark and metrics.}
We evaluate on HotpotQA \citep{yang2018hotpotqa} in a unified-corpus
(distractor-derived) setting: all provided passages are concatenated into a
single index and retrieved from in full. We sample \NUM{N} questions
(target 300--500). We report exact match (EM) and token-level F1 under the
official HotpotQA normalization, an LLM-as-judge accuracy (F1 under-credits
correct answers phrased differently), title-level retrieval precision/recall
against the gold supporting-fact titles, and citation precision (fraction of
cited chunks whose title is a supporting fact).

\paragraph{Retrieval and rerank configuration.}
Dense embeddings: \texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5}. Sparse: Okapi BM25
\citep{robertson2009bm25}. Fusion: RRF with $k=60$
\citep{cormack2009rrf}. Reranker: \texttt{BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3}
cross-encoder. Verifier NLI: \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base}. Each
retrieval arm returns 20 candidates; the fused pool ($\max(4\,k_{\text{final}},20)$
candidates) is reranked to $k_{\text{final}}=5$. Self-consistency uses $k=5$
samples (at vote temperature $0.7$) on the two cheap/local models; the
frontier model is run in a single pass to control cost.

\paragraph{Seeds, determinism, and tracking.}
Every accuracy configuration is run across $\geq 3$ seeds at temperature $0$
(where the provider allows) and reported as mean $\pm$ std with a bootstrap
95\% CI (1000 resamples). MLflow logs, per run: seed, temperature, exact
resolved model version, prompt-template hash, retrieval/rerank/NLI config,
benchmark sample hash, and the Aegis version. Any number in the paper is
reproducible from an exact logged command.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Raw-vs-harnessed comparisons are paired per question: McNemar's test
\citep{mcnemar1947} on EM outcomes, Wilcoxon signed-rank
\citep{wilcoxon1945} and paired-bootstrap deltas on F1. Across the family of
main claims we apply Benjamini--Hochberg FDR correction
\citep{benjamini1995fdr}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state limits explicitly, as they bound every claim above.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Token and latency overhead.} The harness adds a structuring
  call, up to $k$ voting samples, and a verification retry per question;
  reported cost is net of this overhead, but latency grows accordingly and the
  ``cheap+harness'' point is not free.
  \item \textbf{Domain.} All results are Wikipedia-domain multi-hop QA. We do
  not claim the effect transfers to open-web, code, or long-document settings.
  \item \textbf{Single-benchmark generalization.} Conclusions rest primarily on
  HotpotQA; CRAG and MuSiQue are discussed but not the basis of the headline
  numbers.
  \item \textbf{Train-data contamination.} Modern LLMs may have seen HotpotQA
  during pretraining, which can inflate closed-book and raw-pipeline accuracy
  and understate the retrieval contribution; we do not fully control for this.
  \item \textbf{Unvalidated LLM judge.} The LLM-as-judge metric is not
  validated against human labels, so it should be read as a complementary
  signal to EM/F1, not ground truth.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Aegis shows that a fixed, model-agnostic reliability harness --- typed query
structuring, injection defense, hybrid retrieval under a citation contract,
and NLI groundedness verification with self-consistency --- can recover a
substantial fraction of the reliability gap between cheap and frontier models
on grounded multi-hop QA, while measurably reducing prompt-injection success.
Because every table and figure is regenerated from MLflow-logged runs, the
result set re-derives itself whenever an experiment is re-run. We release the
evaluation CLI, the poisoned-corpus generator, and the ablation study.
